# Banking Fundamentals: A Comprehensive Study Guide

## 1. Introduction to Banks

A **bank** is a financial institution licensed to receive deposits and make loans. Banks may also provide financial services such as wealth management, currency exchange, and safe deposit boxes. There are several types of banks including retail banks, commercial banks, and investment banks.

### Key Functions of Banks
- **Deposit-taking**: Accept deposits from customers
- **Lending**: Provide loans to individuals and businesses
- **Payment services**: Facilitate transactions and money transfers
- **Investment services**: Wealth management and investment advice
- **Foreign exchange**: Currency exchange services

## 2. Types of Banks

### 2.1 Retail Banks (Commercial Banks)
- Serve individual customers and small businesses
- Provide checking accounts, savings accounts, mortgages, personal loans
- Examples: Chase, Bank of America, Wells Fargo

### 2.2 Investment Banks
- Focus on large corporations and institutional clients
- Provide services like underwriting, mergers & acquisitions, securities trading
- Examples: Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan Chase

### 2.3 Central Banks
- Government institutions that manage monetary policy
- Control money supply and interest rates
- Examples: Federal Reserve (US), European Central Bank (ECB), Bank of England

### 2.4 Credit Unions
- Not-for-profit financial cooperatives
- Owned by members who share a common bond
- Typically offer better rates than traditional banks

### 2.5 Online Banks
- Operate without physical branches
- Lower overhead costs allow for higher interest rates and lower fees
- Examples: Ally Bank, Chime, Marcus by Goldman Sachs

## 3. Banking Products and Services

### 3.1 Deposit Accounts

#### Checking Accounts
- Used for everyday transactions
- Provides debit card and check-writing privileges
- Usually pays little to no interest

#### Savings Accounts
- Designed for storing money over longer periods
- Pays interest on deposits
- Limited number of withdrawals per month

#### Certificates of Deposit (CDs)
- Fixed-term deposits with higher interest rates
- Early withdrawal penalties apply
- Terms range from 3 months to 5+ years

#### Money Market Accounts
- Hybrid between checking and savings
- Higher interest rates than regular savings
- May require higher minimum balance

### 3.2 Lending Products

#### Personal Loans
- Unsecured loans for various personal expenses
- Fixed interest rate and repayment term
- Amounts typically range from $1,000 to $50,000

#### Mortgages
- Loans secured by real estate property
- Long-term (15-30 years typically)
- Types: Fixed-rate, adjustable-rate (ARM), FHA, VA

#### Auto Loans
- Secured loans for vehicle purchases
- Typically 3-7 year terms
- Interest rates based on credit score and loan term

#### Credit Cards
- Revolving credit line
- Interest charged on unpaid balances
- May offer rewards, cashback, or travel benefits

#### Home Equity Loans/Lines of Credit (HELOC)
- Secured by home equity
- Lower interest rates than unsecured loans
- Can be used for home improvements, debt consolidation, etc.

### 3.3 Investment and Advisory Services
- Wealth management
- Retirement planning (401k, IRA)
- Brokerage services
- Financial advisory

## 4. Key Banking Concepts

### 4.1 Interest Rates

**Simple Interest Formula:**
```
I = P × r × t
```
Where:
- I = Interest
- P = Principal amount
- r = Annual interest rate (as decimal)
- t = Time in years

**Compound Interest Formula:**
```
A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt)
```
Where:
- A = Final amount
- P = Principal
- r = Annual interest rate
- n = Number of times interest is compounded per year
- t = Time in years

**Example:**
If you deposit $10,000 in a savings account with 5% annual interest compounded monthly for 3 years:
```
A = 10000(1 + 0.05/12)^(12×3)
A = 10000(1.004167)^36
A = $11,616.17
```

### 4.2 Annual Percentage Rate (APR) vs Annual Percentage Yield (APY)

**APR (Annual Percentage Rate)**
- The annual rate charged for borrowing
- Does not account for compounding
- Used for loans and credit cards

**APY (Annual Percentage Yield)**
- The effective annual rate of return
- Accounts for compounding
- Used for savings and investment accounts

### 4.3 Fractional Reserve Banking

Banks are only required to keep a fraction of deposits as reserves and can lend out the rest.

**Reserve Requirement Ratio:**
```
Reserve Ratio = Required Reserves / Total Deposits
```

**Example:**
- Total Deposits: $1,000,000
- Reserve Requirement: 10%
- Required Reserves: $100,000
- Available to Lend: $900,000

**Money Multiplier:**
```
Money Multiplier = 1 / Reserve Ratio
```

For a 10% reserve ratio:
```
Money Multiplier = 1 / 0.10 = 10
```

This means banks can create up to 10 times the initial deposit through the lending process.

## 5. Banking Regulations and Safety

### 5.1 FDIC Insurance (United States)
- Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation
- Insures deposits up to $250,000 per depositor, per bank
- Protects against bank failure

### 5.2 Basel Accords
- International banking regulations
- Set minimum capital requirements
- Risk management standards
- Basel III implemented after 2008 financial crisis

### 5.3 Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Know Your Customer (KYC)
- Banks must verify customer identities
- Monitor for suspicious transactions
- Report large cash transactions (>$10,000 in US)
- Prevent terrorist financing and money laundering

### 5.4 Consumer Protection Laws
- Truth in Lending Act (TILA)
- Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA)
- Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA)
- Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform Act

## 7. Financial Ratios and Bank Performance Metrics

### 7.1 Return on Assets (ROA)
```
ROA = Net Income / Total Assets
```
Measures how efficiently a bank uses its assets to generate profit.

### 7.2 Return on Equity (ROE)
```
ROE = Net Income / Shareholders' Equity
```
Measures profitability relative to shareholders' equity.

### 7.3 Net Interest Margin (NIM)
```
NIM = (Investment Income - Interest Expenses) / Average Earning Assets
```
Measures the difference between interest earned and interest paid.

### 7.4 Efficiency Ratio
```
Efficiency Ratio = Non-Interest Expenses / Revenue
```
Lower ratio indicates better efficiency (typical target: below 60%).

### 7.5 Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR)
```
CAR = (Tier 1 Capital + Tier 2 Capital) / Risk-Weighted Assets
```
Measures a bank's capital relative to its risk-weighted assets.

## 8. Practice Problems

### Problem 1: Simple Interest Calculation
**Question:** You deposit $5,000 in a savings account that pays 3% simple interest annually. How much interest will you earn after 4 years?

**Solution:**
```
I = P × r × t
I = 5000 × 0.03 × 4
I = $600
```

### Problem 2: Compound Interest
**Question:** You invest $8,000 in a CD that pays 4% annual interest compounded quarterly for 5 years. What will be the final amount?

**Solution:**
```
A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt)
A = 8000(1 + 0.04/4)^(4×5)
A = 8000(1.01)^20
A = 8000 × 1.2202
A = $9,761.60
```

### Problem 3: APY Calculation
**Question:** A bank advertises 6% APR compounded monthly. What is the APY?

**Solution:**
```
APY = (1 + r/n)^n - 1
APY = (1 + 0.06/12)^12 - 1
APY = (1.005)^12 - 1
APY = 1.06168 - 1
APY = 0.06168 or 6.168%
```

### Problem 4: Loan Payment Calculation
**Question:** You take out a $20,000 auto loan at 5% annual interest for 4 years. What is your monthly payment?

**Solution:** Using the loan payment formula:
```
M = P[r(1+r)^n] / [(1+r)^n - 1]
```
Where:
- P = $20,000 (principal)
- r = 0.05/12 = 0.004167 (monthly rate)
- n = 4 × 12 = 48 (number of payments)

```
M = 20000[0.004167(1.004167)^48] / [(1.004167)^48 - 1]
M = 20000[0.004167 × 1.2209] / [1.2209 - 1]
M = 20000 × 0.005086 / 0.2209
M = $460.41
```

### Problem 5: Money Multiplier
**Question:** If the reserve requirement is 8%, and a customer deposits $50,000, how much money can potentially be created through the banking system?

**Solution:**
```
Money Multiplier = 1 / Reserve Ratio
Money Multiplier = 1 / 0.08 = 12.5

Total Money Creation = Initial Deposit × Money Multiplier
Total Money Creation = $50,000 × 12.5 = $625,000
```

## 9. Study Tips and Key Takeaways

### Essential Concepts to Master:
1. ✓ Understand the difference between APR and APY
2. ✓ Know how to calculate simple and compound interest
3. ✓ Understand fractional reserve banking and money creation
4. ✓ Familiarize yourself with different types of banks and their functions
5. ✓ Learn key banking regulations and consumer protections
6. ✓ Stay informed about digital banking and fintech trends
7. ✓ Understand financial ratios used to assess bank performance

### Common Mistakes to Avoid:
- Confusing APR with APY
- Forgetting to convert annual rates to monthly rates for loan calculations
- Not accounting for compounding frequency in interest calculations
- Overlooking fees when comparing bank products
- Ignoring the impact of inflation on savings
